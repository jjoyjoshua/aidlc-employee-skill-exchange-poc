Rating stars

A trainer's average rating, shown on every search result card and at the top of the trainer panel (REQ-025). The glyphs are decoration; the number is the information. That ordering is what makes the component readable without colour and readable by a screen reader (NFR-003).

Every average this component has to render

★★★★★ 5.0 · 4 ratings Rated 5.0 out of 5, from 4 ratings ★★★★☆ 4.6 · 12 ratings ★★★★☆ 4.1 · 7 ratings ★★★☆☆ 3.2 · 5 ratings ★★☆☆☆ 2.0 · 1 rating

The count is never omitted. "4.6" from twelve people and "5.0" from one are different facts, and a learner deciding between two colleagues needs both. Singular and plural are written out — "1 rating", not "1 ratings".

The desaturation check

★★★★☆ 4.6 · 12 ratings ☆ Not rated yet

The same two examples with all colour removed. Rated and unrated stay tellable apart, because the difference was never carried by colour — it is carried by the numeral and by the sentence (NFR-003).

What a screen reader is told

<span class="rating">
  <span class="stars" aria-hidden="true">★★★★☆</span>
  <span class="avg">4.6</span>
  <span class="count">· 12 ratings</span>
</span>

The star glyphs are aria-hidden, so the row is announced as "4.6 · 12 ratings" rather than as five separate star characters. On a card, the surrounding markup supplies the full sentence: "Rated 4.6 out of 5, from 12 ratings."
