Notification row — SCR-009

The row is shared by both presentations: the bell panel and the /notifications page render the same component at different widths. Everything it must distinguish — eight kinds, read from unread, live from stale — it distinguishes with an icon and a word, not a colour.

Unread carries three signals at once — the dot, the heavier sentence, the tint. The read row keeps the dot's footprint so nothing slides sideways the moment it is read. The visible time is relative; the exact local time lives in the title and the accessible name, because "2h ago" means nothing to someone back from lunch.

The first row has just been activated — shown here with its focus ring, the state a keyboard user sees. If the read-marking call fails the navigation still happens and the dot silently returns; nothing is announced, because by then the employee is on another screen and a failed bookkeeping call is not their problem.

Kept and annotated, never deleted. An employee who remembers being told something must still be able to find that they were told it — a row that vanishes makes the product look like it lied. It is a <div> rather than an <a>, so it leaves the tab order along with its destination.

"Expired" (N5) deliberately does not reuse the declined wording or icon, even though REQ-018 calls it an auto-rejection: the trainer never saw the request, and telling a learner that a colleague declined them would be untrue. The three that sit next to each other and mean opposite things — approved, declined, expired — are the reason every kind carries a written label.
