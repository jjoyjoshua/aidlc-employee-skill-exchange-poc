Trainer result card

One colleague in the "Find a trainer" results (SCR-006). Everything a learner needs to decide whether to open the panel sits on the card: who they are, where they sit, how they have been rated (REQ-025), and what they teach at what level (REQ-010).

The card is one link with one accessible name, so a screen-reader user hears the whole colleague as a single item rather than tabbing through five fragments. Proficiency is written out on every chip — never a colour, never a count of dots (NFR-003).

With a skill searched — the matching chip is marked

PM Priya M. Finance · Financial Analyst ★★★★☆ 4.6 · 12 ratings Teaches ✓ Excel · Expert SQL · Intermediate

When a skill is searched, the chip that matched carries a tick as well as the colour change, and the accessible name says "matching your search". The other skills stay visible — a learner looking for Excel is often glad to discover the same colleague also teaches SQL.

First load — skeleton

The skeleton occupies the height a real card will, so nothing re-flows when the response arrives. The whole block is aria-hidden; the list region carries aria-busy="true" instead, so a screen reader is told "loading" once rather than reading four rows of nothing.

Dimmed while a refinement loads

Cards lose their tab stop and their pointer events while dimmed, so a learner cannot open a colleague who is about to be filtered out from under them. The count line above the list carries the "Searching…" indicator — the dim alone is never the only signal.

Chips wrap rather than truncate, and the card grows. At POC scale nobody lists enough skills for this to run away; if that changes, capping the chips with "+3 more" is the move, and it belongs in a revision of this preview rather than as an undocumented runtime rule.
